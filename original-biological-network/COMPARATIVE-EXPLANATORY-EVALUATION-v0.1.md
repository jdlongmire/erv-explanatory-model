# Comparative Explanatory Evaluation v0.1

**Date:** 18 November 2026  
**Status:** Qualitative, preregistered-style comparative framework; no new locus-level validation or likelihood calculation.

## 1. Models under evaluation

**C: Conventional evolutionary reconstruction.** Retroviral infection and integration, inheritance through common ancestry, lineage-specific integration, host-range shifts and horizontal transmission, recombination, deletion, gene conversion, and functional co-option. These are separately evidenced mechanisms. The precise history at a given locus remains inferential.

**N: Original Biological Network.** An initially functionally mature biosphere with proposed beneficial information-transfer systems and potentially initially placed functional modules, followed by viral transmission, vertical inheritance, diversification, modification and loss. Mechanisms shared with C are treated as shared explanatory resources, not distinctive confirmation of N.

**H: Case-by-case composite.** Some features originate in initial architecture, while others arise through later infection and inheritance. H is a bookkeeping option, not a separate confirmed model; unless initial-placement rules are independently constrained it risks explaining any result post hoc.

## 2. Scoring principles

1. **Observation:** state exactly what was measured, in which species and assembly, with primary provenance.
2. **Mechanistic adequacy:** identify the specific causal chain; do not credit the model for a mechanism it does not specify.
3. **Independent constraint:** count the degree to which the model predicted the observation before learning it.
4. **Auxiliary burden:** record unverified assumptions, especially unconstrained initial placement, ad hoc deletion or undocumented cross-host transfer.
5. **Discrimination:** mark an observation as *shared compatibility* when both models can explain it.
6. **Counterevidence:** retain observations that strain each model, especially orthologous loci and shared derived mutations.
7. **Uncertainty:** no numerical probabilities or rankings absent calibrated priors, sampling and likelihood functions.

## 4. Provisional assessment

- **Logical coherence:** N remains coherent under its declared initial-state assumptions.
- **Biological component feasibility:** viral exchange, integration, inheritance, modification and functional recruitment are independently observed.
- **Historical original-network coordination:** unverified; no direct evidence of a coordinated original system has been established.
- **Explanatory fit:** C currently has stronger, independently established explanations for precise orthologous integrations and shared derived sequence changes. N accommodates several observations using mechanisms also admitted by C; this does not demonstrate equivalent explanatory support.
- **Relative model preference:** **not formally determined**. The evidence reviewed does not justify numerical Bayes factors or a claim that N is superior.

## 5. Distinguishing tests, predeclared

**T1: Independently specified initial functional modules.** Specify a restricted module class and predicted host compatibility pattern from functional requirements *before* candidate selection. Compare hit rates and false positives against conventional viral-evolution expectations.

**T2: Exact locus and phylogenetic congruence.** At preselected ERV loci, evaluate flanks, target-site duplications, shared mutations, structural variants and host-tree congruence. Repeated nested inheritance patterns strengthen C and constrain any independent-initial-placement component of N.

**T3: Nonorthologous transfer histories.** For preselected PTERV1 or analogous families, evaluate viral phylogeny, inferred host transfers, integration dates, host range and ecology. Horizontal transfer alone supports neither original-network provenance nor coordinated original design.

**T4: Symmetric cross-lineage A/B.** Use the same detection sensitivity and species coverage for both targets. Treat assembly gaps and unsampled populations as unresolved; do not reclassify as absence.

**T5: Functional-system integration.** Test whether putative cross-lineage modules exhibit coordinated specificity, targeting and regulation beyond what conventional co-option and selection predict. Require independently registered quantitative expectations before claiming discriminatory support.

## 6. Model failure and revision rules

- Reject a specific initial-placement explanation if validated orthology and shared derived mutations contradict its stated independent-origin prediction.
- Revise or abandon a claimed universal original benefit if the claim is operationalized and repeatedly contradicted; an unfalsifiable universal assertion cannot receive empirical corroboration.
- Do not rescue every anomalous locus by assigning unrestricted initial placement, transfer or deletion.
- A verified cross-lineage pattern is evidence of a distribution; its causal interpretation requires additional mechanism-specific evidence.
- Preserve failed searches, negative cases and provenance, and do not cross-import results from firewalled research programmes.

## 7. Milestone disposition

**Conclusion:** The Original Biological Network retains *preliminary plausibility* as a biologically informed proposal. It has not attained comparative explanatory parity or historical confirmation. Conventional reconstruction currently enjoys stronger direct support for orthologous ERV inheritance. The next substantive improvement requires prospective, constrained predictions and real sequence validation.
