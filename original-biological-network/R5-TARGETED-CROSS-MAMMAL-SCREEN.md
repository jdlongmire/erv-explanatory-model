# Targeted cross-mammal ERV screening: R5

**Date:** 18 November 2026. **Status:** Primary-paper evidence screening. No sequence-level comparison performed.

## Exact research target
- **A:** human + at least one non-primate mammal share a defined ERV signature, while chimpanzee, bonobo, gorilla, and orangutan lack that same signature.
- **B:** one or more nonhuman great apes + at least one non-primate mammal share a defined ERV signature, while human lacks it.
- Separate **family homology**, **subfamily**, **orthologous genomic insertion**, and **functional receptor compatibility**. Absence must be tested at the same resolution as presence.

The proposed original network permits later transmission, including post-Fall transmission in its theological historical framing. None of these patterns alone dates the event.

## Primary evidence notes

**CERV1/PTERV1 and CERV2:** Comparative chimpanzee genomic research identified both as families lacking human orthologues. CERV2 PCR plus Southern hybridization supported presence in chimpanzee, bonobo, gorilla and Old World monkeys and absence in human and orangutan. The same paper separated 31 human-positive/chimp-negative regions into eight human insertions and 23 chimpanzee deletions. This is important evidence against inferring insertion solely from presence/absence.

**ERV-Fc:** The 2016 mammalian survey inferred repeated cross-species transmission and independently acquired genomic lineages, including human, chimpanzee and dog. At family level, ERV-Fc cannot meet the required exclusion pattern. A narrower sublineage test would require defined sequence markers and full ape coverage.

**Receptor compatibility:** A 2025 primary experimental study identified human SLC52A2 as a receptor for reconstructed CERV1 entry, and found receptor/interference overlap with porcine endogenous retrovirus. This directly demonstrates cross-species *cell-entry compatibility* relevant to transmission feasibility. It does not establish pig–ape shared ERV genomic insertion or the historical origin of CERV1. Host receptor use and endogenization are different propositions.

**PTERV1 retroviral similarity:** The original 2005 study reported protein similarity to murine, feline, porcine and baboon retroviruses. Similarity to broad gammaretrovirus proteins is insufficient to classify a non-primate mammal as positive for the exact PTERV1 family.

## Result

**Full A: UNVERIFIED. Full B: UNVERIFIED** when the non-ape comparator is a **non-primate mammal**.

**Confirmed narrower observation:** Ape-positive, Old World monkey-positive, human-negative CERV1/PTERV1 and CERV2 distributions.

**New mechanistic observation:** CERV1 and PERV can share entry-related receptor biology, showing a possible cross-mammal compatibility mechanism, without proving shared endogenization.
