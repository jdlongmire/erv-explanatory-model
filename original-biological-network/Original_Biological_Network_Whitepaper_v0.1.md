# Original Biological Network
## A Functional-Maturity Hypothesis for Viral Systems and Genomic Architecture

**Research whitepaper v0.1 | 18 November 2026**  
**Status:** Exploratory hypothesis; not peer reviewed; no new sequence-level findings claimed  
**Framework:** Designed Functional Maturity (Designist initial conditions)  
**Research standard:** Explanatory plausibility, with explicit empirical constraints

## Abstract

This paper explores whether an originally functionally mature biosphere could have included a beneficial, regulated biological information network, incorporating viral or virus-like delivery systems and initially functional genomic architecture. It proposes that some sequences presently classified as endogenous retroviruses (ERVs), or more cautiously ERV-like elements, could represent original functional components, modified descendants of those components, or later historical integrations. The proposal allows diversification within created kinds and cross-kind viral transmission. It does not dispute the existence of retroviral integration, heritable insertion, mutation, natural selection, or horizontal transmission. Rather, it questions whether all present ERV-like structures must be assigned the same kind of pre-observation history. Evidence of gene-transfer agents, essential retroviral-derived host genes, lineage-specific ERV expansions, and orthologous primate ERVs establishes important mechanisms and stringent constraints. The aim is to develop a coherent and empirically compatible candidate explanation, while identifying observations that would weaken or require revision of the hypothesis.

## 1. The question and the standard of inference

**Research question:** Could an originally beneficial biological information network, combined with subsequent inheritance, cross-kind viral transfer, diversification and degradation, plausibly account for a nontrivial subset of present ERV-like genomic patterns?

The question distinguishes four levels:

1. **Observation:** sequence identity, locus position, flanking homology, insertion boundaries, target-site duplications, substitutions, expression and phenotype.
2. **Demonstrated mechanism:** retroviral integration, vertical inheritance, mutation, recombination, selection, viral transmission and regulated gene delivery.
3. **Historical reconstruction:** an inferred sequence of integration, inheritance, loss, co-option and divergence events.
4. **Initial-condition premise:** whether the original biosphere contained preconfigured functional genomic elements and beneficial delivery systems.

The first two levels are constrained by direct measurement and experiments. The third is assessed through historical inference. The fourth is the distinctive point of departure for Designed Functional Maturity.

**Logical caution:** A mechanism known to produce a structure does not deductively establish that every instance of the structure was produced by that mechanism. **Scientific caution:** The ability to imagine an original structure does not supply evidence that it existed. The present hypothesis must therefore specify physically and biologically credible initial configurations and mechanisms.

The present objective is *plausibility*: coherence, causal feasibility, empirical compatibility, and disciplined treatment of alternatives. The paper does not claim comparative explanatory superiority.

## 2. Designed Functional Maturity

Designed Functional Maturity proposes that a created biosphere began in a working state with the structures and relationships necessary for its intended operation. A mature river system illustrates the distinction: the existence of a channel does not logically require that every part of the channel was formed by an antecedent interval of ordinary erosion. The hypothesis concerns the **initial configuration**, while allowing real subsequent processes to modify that configuration.

An analogous geophysical illustration is the initial radiogenic heat inventory. A viable planetary thermal regime imposes constraints on initial heat sources, but the required amount and isotopic composition cannot simply be asserted. Measured daughter products, mineral systems and heat flow still demand accounting. Nor is a unique radiogenic budget for life established. The lesson for genomics is methodological: proposed functional initial conditions must satisfy independent physical and observational constraints.

In this framework, a genomic arrangement that resembles the result of retroviral integration might be an original functional feature, a historical integration, or a subsequently modified element. Determining which explanation is warranted requires more than naming the sequence a “scar” or a “design trace.” Both terms carry historical interpretations.

## 3. Foundational propositions and status

**P1. Original functional maturity (framework premise; UNCERTAIN empirically).** The initial biosphere possessed working genomic, organismal and ecological systems.

**P2. Beneficial viral systems (historical conjecture; UNCERTAIN).** Viral or virus-like mechanisms initially performed beneficial roles, potentially including regulated transfer of genetic information. The strong universal claim that *all* original viruses were beneficial is a foundational proposal, not an observation established by present genomic data.

**P3. Original genomic architecture (historical conjecture; UNCERTAIN).** Some ERV-like elements could have existed as components of initial functional genomes without a preceding infection event.

**P4. Diversification within created kinds (permitted mechanism).** Mutation, recombination, selection, drift and inheritance can produce branching variation within kinds. The boundaries of “kind” must be specified independently for empirical use.

**P5. Cross-kind viral transmission (permitted mechanism).** Viral material may enter organisms across kind boundaries and, if integrated into germ cells, become heritable. Cross-species transmission is independently documented; “cross-kind” additionally requires a defensible kind assignment.

**P6. Modification and degradation (historical conjecture).** Some originally beneficial components may lose regulatory control, targeting specificity or host compatibility and become pathogenic or functionally diminished. Other elements may remain useful, acquire new functions, or be lost.

The propositions are not all equally supported. Treating them as one established conclusion would obscure the central research question.

## 4. A possible original network

A functioning information-transfer network would plausibly require:

- **Payload handling:** encoding, selection, protection and transport of transferable genetic material.
- **Recipient recognition:** cellular targeting, host-range control and delivery.
- **Expression and integration control:** regulated transient expression or stable incorporation where appropriate.
- **Compatibility and quality control:** mechanisms limiting harmful insertion, runaway replication and incompatible payloads.
- **Ecological governance:** constraints on transmission rates and interactions across organismal communities.

These are *functional requirements for the proposed architecture*, not demonstrated features of an ancestral universal system. The model does not require every virus to have performed the same task. The forest analogy depicts distinct branching lineages connected by information-transfer pathways; it is explanatory imagery, not evidence of a historical network.

Possible failure modes include regulatory escape, excessive replication, harmful integration, payload corruption, broadened host range and ecological imbalance. None establishes that present pathogenic viruses actually arose through this proposed direction of change.

## 5. Empirical anchors

### 5.1 Regulated virus-like genetic exchange

Lang and Beatty (2000) studied the *Rhodobacter capsulatus* gene-transfer agent, a bacteriophage-like system involved in genetic exchange. This demonstrates that virus-like structures can participate in regulated DNA transfer. Their study interpreted the element in a prophage-derived context; it cannot be cited as evidence that all viruses began as beneficial transfer systems.

### 5.2 Essential function of retroviral-derived sequences

Dupressoir et al. (2009) demonstrated that disrupting mouse *syncytin-A*, a retrovirus-derived envelope gene, severely impairs placentation and embryonic survival. This is strong evidence of present biological utility. It is consistent with both functional recruitment following infection and a broader hypothesis involving functional viral architecture. Present benefit alone does not establish original function or design.

### 5.3 Discontinuous ERV distribution and independent insertion

Yohn et al. (2005) examined PTERV1 sequences in African great apes and Old World monkeys, finding lineage-specific expansions and largely nonorthologous integration sites. Humans and orangutans lacked the surveyed PTERV1 pattern. This demonstrates that host relationships and ERV distributions need not be congruent at every locus, and supports independent viral acquisition. It does not negate the evidence for vertical inheritance at other loci.

### 5.4 Orthologous insertions and inherited patterns

Johnson and Coffin (1999) used endogenous retroviral sequence relationships to reconstruct primate phylogenies. Orthologous genomic position, shared flanking sequences, insertion boundaries, target-site duplications and shared derived mutations can jointly provide strong evidence for ancestral integration and inheritance. A competing initial-configuration account must explain their **joint pattern**, not only their individual possibility.

## 6. What is actually shared?

Different uses of “shared ERV signature” carry different historical implications:

| Evidence class | What it establishes | Historical question |
|---|---|---|
| Related ERV family | Sequence homology or recognizable retroviral architecture | Common viral lineage, transfer, ancestry or other origin? |
| Similar protein domain | Shared molecular architecture | Homology, functional constraint or convergence? |
| Comparable biological function | Functionally similar role | Independent recruitment, shared inheritance or proposed initial function? |
| Orthologous locus | Corresponding sequence at homologous genomic location | Shared inheritance, recurrent integration, original placement or complex loss? |
| Matching insertion boundaries and target-site duplication | Detailed integration-like molecular signature | Which mechanism best explains exact correspondence? |
| Shared derived substitutions or disabling mutations | Correlated sequence history | Inheritance, recurrent change, gene conversion or proposed original state? |

The hypothesis must never use family-level similarity as though it demonstrated an identical insertion.

## 7. Competing histories and explanatory obligations

**H1: Conventional historical reconstruction.** Retroviral infections enter germlines; insertions are vertically inherited, mutated, lost or co-opted; horizontal transmission and independent integrations also occur. This model has substantial independent mechanistic and comparative genomic support.

**H2: Original network plus later processes.** Some initial ERV-like structures are original functional components; some arise from initially beneficial delivery systems; subsequent diversification, cross-kind transmission, mutation, selection, integration and degradation generate the observed mosaic.

**H3: Mixed case-by-case reconstruction.** Particular loci may require different origins. H3 is descriptively flexible and therefore scientifically useful only when explicit constraints prevent every observation from being explained after the fact.

A plausible H2 must explain why some loci appear precisely orthologous and share correlated mutations, why others are lineage-specific and nonorthologous, and why some viral-derived sequences are functional while many are disrupted. The initial-design option must not be used as an unrestricted escape clause.

An explicit challenge for H2 is **directionality**: functional recruitment of retroviral sequences is observed, while a globally beneficial original viral ecosystem has not been independently reconstructed. Another is **shared neutral or disabling variation**, which often carries historical information not readily explained by present functional requirements.

## 8. Targeted comparative-genomics question

**Target pattern:** an ERV-related signature present in humans and at least one non-ape mammal, absent from chimpanzees, bonobos, gorillas and orangutans.

**Current status:** No exact qualifying locus has been independently verified in this project. Published examples of broadly shared ERV families and lineage-specific integrations are relevant but do not establish the exact target.

**Study protocol:**

1. Define separately a family-level match, high-identity sequence match and exact orthologous insertion. Register thresholds before analysis.
2. Select candidate human loci with accession, assembly build, genomic coordinates, strand, LTR boundaries and source publication.
3. Search selected non-ape mammalian assemblies for family homology, then assess flanking synteny for any candidate orthology.
4. Examine chimpanzee, bonobo, gorilla and orangutan orthologous regions; distinguish a true empty site from an assembly gap, rearrangement or deletion.
5. Confirm target-site duplications, insertion junctions, sequence identity, derived mutations and possible contamination.
6. Evaluate alternatives including recurrent infection, independent targeting, ancestral polymorphism, incomplete lineage sorting, deletion, gene conversion and horizontal transfer.
7. Report confirmed positives, confirmed negatives, unresolved loci and failures, including the possibility that no qualifying locus exists.

A family-level human/non-ape match with ape absence would be compatible with horizontal viral transfer already recognized in conventional evolutionary biology. An identical orthologous locus would demand much more detailed explanation. Neither outcome alone would prove initial design.

## 9. Plausibility gates

| Gate | Minimum showing | Failure or revision trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Logical coherence | No mutually incompatible foundational propositions | Contradictory initial-condition claims |
| Mechanistic feasibility | A physically and molecularly credible delivery architecture | Requires unknown capabilities with no constrained mechanism |
| Empirical compatibility | Accounts for boundaries, orthology, mutations and function without denying measurements | Repeated, unaddressed contradictions with verified loci |
| Explanatory discipline | Initial design, transfer, inheritance and loss assigned by stated criteria | Unrestricted post hoc origin assignments |
| Source integrity | Primary papers, assemblies, coordinates and confidence recorded | Untraceable or misrepresented evidence |
| Research utility | At least one informative test or discriminating expectation | Cannot distinguish the hypothesis from unrestricted accommodation |

Passing these gates would support *candidate plausibility*, not historical confirmation. The stronger claim that H2 explains evidence better than H1 requires comparative predictions, likelihood assessment and explicit complexity costs.

## 10. Research priorities

**R1. Source audit:** verify bibliographic records, DOI metadata, methods and supplemental datasets for the foundational papers.

**R2. Locus inventory:** curate human-specific ERV loci and broadly distributed mammalian ERV families with clear family-versus-locus distinctions.

**R3. Ape-negative search:** independently inspect homologous ape loci and non-ape matches using assembly-aware sequence comparisons.

**R4. Original-function constraints:** investigate molecular features that an originally regulated network would require, and whether such features are independently observable.

**R5. Competing explanations:** construct a transparent per-locus comparison including ancestral integration, independent infection, horizontal transfer, loss, initial placement and unresolved alternatives.

**R6. Revision ledger:** register specific observations that would strengthen, weaken or narrow the original-network proposal.

## 11. Conclusions

The Original Biological Network Hypothesis offers a candidate account of an originally functionally mature biosphere in which viral systems may have performed beneficial information-transfer roles. It permits real evolutionary diversification within created kinds and cross-kind viral integration, while proposing that some present ERV-like genomic structures might reflect original functional architecture.

The proposal is logically coherent at the level described here, but its historical claims remain **UNCERTAIN**. Demonstrated viral-derived functions and gene-transfer mechanisms establish biological precedents; they do not establish the proposed original state. Conversely, current retroviral integration mechanisms and strong evidence of inherited orthologous ERVs do not logically require every ERV-like structure to share an identical pre-observation history.

The next scientific obligation is to constrain the hypothesis with locus-level evidence and explicit causal criteria. A plausible explanation must account for inconvenient observations as carefully as congenial ones. No specific ERV locus has yet been shown by this project to require original genomic placement.
