# Original Biological Network: Formal Causal Model v0.1

**Date:** 18 November 2026  
**Status:** Exploratory hypothesis specification. Plausibility milestone, not historical confirmation.

## 1. Domain and definitions

A **functional-maturity initial condition** is a hypothesized original biological configuration containing complete, viable ecological and genomic functions from its beginning. It is a historical premise; it is not derived from ERV data.

An **original biological network** is a proposed initially regulated system of information exchange among biological entities, potentially including virus-like carriers, host compatibility constraints, and functional genomic modules.

A **created kind** is a proposed initial lineage boundary. Its membership must be operationally specified using evidence independent of the ERV pattern under test. Until then, “cross-kind” is a provisional model term; species-level or clade-level observations should be reported using standard taxonomy.

A **genomic signature** is a defined sequence feature with specified detection criteria. Family homology, function, and orthologous insertion are different signature types and must not be conflated.

## 2. Initial-state propositions

- **I1 (hypothesized):** The original biosphere was functionally mature and ecologically integrated.
- **I2 (hypothesized):** Some originally present virus-like entities served beneficial information-handling functions.
- **I3 (hypothesized):** Some shared genomic modules were part of initial functional architecture, potentially independently present in distinct original lineages.
- **I4 (constraint):** Initial modules cannot be assigned arbitrarily to every presently observed insertion. A proposed initial placement must be supported by an independently specified functional or distributional expectation.
- **I5 (constraint):** Original lineage boundaries and permitted initial exchange partners must be defined before scoring selected ERV distributions.

The initial state is a parameterized historical proposal, not an unrestricted explanatory escape hatch.

| Mechanism | Biological status | Role in model | Limits |

## 4. Causal sequence

**S0: Initial configuration.** Hypothesized functionally mature biosphere, including viable organisms, host-specific genomic architectures, and regulated information-transfer functions.

**S1: Routine operation.** Network components perform bounded exchange or regulation. Specific transfer pathways and target preferences must eventually be specified.

**S2: Diversification.** Organisms diversify; vertically inherited genomic elements change and become lineage-specific.

**S3: Cross-lineage exposure and integration.** Viral lineages transmit where host range and ecological contact permit; insertion histories can be nonorthologous.

**S4: Alteration and loss.** Mutations, structural variation, silencing and deletions change distribution and function. Proposed degradation is a historical interpretation requiring locus-specific evidence.

**S5: Present observation.** Sequence families, syntenic loci, regulatory functions, shared disabling changes and missing/ambiguous assemblies are measured.

The model permits multiple pathways to a present signature. Every proposed explanation must name its pathway and evidence, rather than assigning any inconvenient pattern to initial design.

## 5. Two complementary causal pathways

**Initial placement pathway:** initial functional genomic architecture → vertical inheritance/diversification → lineage-specific modification/loss → observed genomic signature.

**Transmission pathway:** initially functional transfer capability (hypothesized) → later viral transmission/integration (biologically established mechanism) → inheritance and change → observed genomic signature.

The second pathway's observed modern mechanism does not independently establish the first event's historical origin.

## 6. Evidentiary obligations and disconfirming outcomes

- **Shared orthologous insertions with common boundaries and derived disabling mutations:** strongly favor inheritance from a shared ancestor for those loci; the model must accept this history or supply an independently testable alternative. Original design alone cannot explain arbitrary shared derived mutations.
- **Shared ERV-family sequences at nonorthologous sites:** compatible with independent integration, horizontal viral transmission, and some original-network proposals; weak discriminator without additional evidence.
- **Functional viral-derived genes:** demonstrate biological feasibility of functional recruitment or maintenance, but do not determine original versus later origin.
- **Bidirectional A/B distributions:** test independently and symmetrically. A = human/non-primate mammal positive, other great apes negative; B = nonhuman great ape/non-primate mammal positive, human negative. Confirm negatives by sensitive genomic and orthologous-region checks.
- **Persistent inability to define created-kind boundaries independently:** prevents a genuinely cross-kind test, though standard taxonomic comparisons remain valid.
- **Need for unlimited ad hoc initial placements or losses:** counts against explanatory adequacy.
- **Repeated predictions that fail under adequate sequence coverage:** require model revision or abandonment of those predictions.

## 7. Plausibility assessment, dated 18 November 2026

| Gate | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Logical coherence | Provisionally passed | No internal contradiction identified in stated propositions |
| Component-level biological feasibility | Supported | Contemporary viral transfer and viral-derived genomic function |
| Empirical compatibility | Supported for selected observations | Nonorthologous PTERV1, functional viral-derived examples |
| Historical network-level feasibility | Preliminary | No demonstration of coordinated original system |
| Comparative explanatory adequacy | Open | No controlled model comparison or likelihood assessment |
| Original historical actuality | Unverified | No direct or discriminating evidence established |

**Milestone claim:** The Original Biological Network is a logically coherent, biologically informed historical proposal with preliminary plausibility. This claim is limited to the feasibility of its component mechanisms and compatibility with selected observations. It is not a finding that the original network existed, that every virus was originally beneficial, or that the hypothesis outperforms common ancestry and conventional viral evolution.

## 8. Model governance

1. Maintain distinct columns for observation, demonstrated mechanism, historical interpretation, and original-state premise.
2. Register locus-specific predictions before selecting favorable examples; retain negative findings.
3. Do not infer created-kind boundaries from the same ERV distributions used to validate them.
4. Prefer direct primary literature and versioned assemblies; mark unverified sequence claims.
5. Compare inheritance, integration, horizontal transmission, ancestral polymorphism, loss and initial placement under consistent evidentiary rules.
6. Preserve independence from parallel research programmes under the documented research firewall.

## 9. Immediate next research questions

1. Which independently constrained functional requirements would an initially regulated biological exchange network have?
2. Which signatures would differ observably from conventional viral transfer and inheritance?
3. What independent criteria can operationalize original lineage boundaries?
4. Can the LINC00662–PTERV1 case be validated with versioned sequence assemblies?
5. Can either complete cross-lineage A or B signature be verified without conflating family similarity and orthologous insertion?
