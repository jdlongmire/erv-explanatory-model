# Functional Genomic Architecture
## A Design-Friendly Interpretation of Formerly Classified “Junk” DNA in the Original Biological Network Hypothesis

**Research whitepaper v0.1**  
**18 November 2026**  
**Status:** Exploratory historical-model development; primary empirical findings distinguished from proposed historical interpretation.

### Abstract

The discovery of regulatory and developmental functions in noncoding and endogenous retrovirus (ERV)-derived DNA warrants a more precise account of genomic function and historical origin. Experiments have demonstrated that specific viral-derived elements are necessary for normal placental development or regulate trophoblast gene expression. Such findings establish that viral-derived genomic material can participate in coordinated biological systems. The Original Biological Network Hypothesis (OBN), within Designed Functional Maturity (DFM), proposes that some information-handling capacities and functional genomic modules may have been present in an initially mature biological system, while later viral transmission, inheritance, integration, co-option, mutation and loss shaped extant distributions. This paper distinguishes demonstrated functionality from hypotheses about origin, evaluates conventional evolutionary explanations fairly, and proposes independently constrained tests. Current evidence supports biological component feasibility and selected observational compatibility. It does not establish original design, the original presence of specific ERV insertions, or comparative explanatory superiority.

### Executive summary

Noncoding DNA is not a single functional category. Some sequences are causally important regulatory elements, some are structural, some are transcribed without demonstrated organism-level benefit, and some may be neutral or deleterious. The historically broad label “junk DNA” obscures these distinctions. Functional discoveries challenge claims that particular regions are dispensable but do not prove that all noncoding DNA is functional.

Specific ERV-derived elements have experimentally demonstrated functions. In mice, loss of syncytin-A disrupts placental syncytiotrophoblast formation. In human trophoblast models, CRISPR perturbations of ERV-derived enhancers affect placental gene expression, and MER50 element deletion impairs syncytialization-related expression and cellular fusion (Dupressoir et al., 2009; Frost et al., 2023; Yuan et al., 2023).

These findings motivate an OBN inquiry into whether original genomic architecture might have included functionally integrated information-transfer modules. The alternative evolutionary explanation, integration followed by co-option and selection, is a substantive, independently researched account. The competing origin histories require discriminating evidence.

### 1. Scope and epistemic categories

**Noncoding DNA** is DNA not translated into protein. It includes regulatory elements, introns, noncoding RNA genes, repetitive DNA and other regions with different functions and histories.

**ERV-derived DNA** comprises sequences associated with retroviral integration and subsequent genomic inheritance or modification. A present-day host function does not determine when the sequence was acquired.

**Function** has multiple operational senses:
- **Biochemical activity:** transcription, binding or chromatin marks; insufficient by itself to establish organism-level benefit.
- **Causal role:** perturbation of a sequence changes a measurable phenotype or gene-expression outcome.
- **Fitness-relevant function:** an effect contributes to survival or reproduction in a specified environment.
- **Historical selected effect:** a feature has been maintained or spread because of its past effect, an inference requiring additional evidence.

**Original functional architecture** is the OBN historical hypothesis that some modules were present as functional components of an initially mature biosphere. This is not an experimental finding.

**Cross-kind transfer after the Fall** is permitted by the OBN's theological historical framework. Neither the Fall nor the date of a specific transfer can be read directly from a present ERV sequence.

### 2. Primary experimental evidence

#### 2.1 Syncytin-A and mammalian placentation

Dupressoir et al. (2009) disrupted mouse syncytin-A, an endogenous retroviral envelope-derived gene, and found severe placental abnormalities associated with failure of trophoblast fusion and embryonic lethality. The intervention provides strong causal evidence of an essential contemporary function. It does not distinguish an originally present designed module from a later integrated element that acquired function.

#### 2.2 Human trophoblast ERV-derived enhancers

Frost et al. (2023) integrated human trophoblast epigenomic profiles and experimental perturbations, identifying ERV-derived regulatory elements that enhance expression of placental genes including *CSF1R* and *PSG5*. An LTR10A element regulated *ENG*, affecting soluble endoglin secretion. The study provides locus-specific functional evidence, while its reported primate-specific patterns are relevant to evolutionary histories of regulatory recruitment.

#### 2.3 MER50 and trophoblast syncytialization

Yuan et al. (2023) reported that MER50-associated enhancers contribute to human trophoblast syncytialization. Deleting selected MER50 elements near *MFSD2A* and *TNFAIP2* attenuated expression and compromised syncytium formation. This supports a causal regulatory role for those elements. The results do not imply that every MER50 copy or all ERV-derived DNA is beneficial.

#### 2.4 The ENCODE interpretation problem

The ENCODE project mapped widespread biochemical activity, stimulating debate about how “functional” DNA should be defined. Doolittle (2013) and Graur (2013) argued that biochemical signatures cannot be equated with selected organism-level function, and that genome-size variation and neutral processes must remain part of the analysis. The debate is pertinent to both design-friendly and conventional interpretations: avoid declaring every transcribed or bound sequence functional.

### 3. Design-friendly historical interpretation

DFM proposes an initially viable and ecologically integrated biosphere. OBN extends this with the possibility of initially regulated biological information-transfer systems and functional genomic modules. Under that hypothesis, some present regulatory architectures could preserve aspects of original organization; others could result from later integration, co-option, and selection.

The model must distinguish at least three pathways:

**P1: Initial functional module → inheritance/diversification → present function.**

**P2: Later viral integration → functional recruitment/selection → present function.**

**P3: Later cross-lineage viral transmission → integration → inheritance/modification → present function or dysfunction.**

All three are conceptually permissible within OBN. The observed existence of function alone does not choose among them. Conventional evolutionary models strongly develop P2 and P3 and often provide locus-specific timing and phylogenetic evidence. OBN's distinctive P1 requires independent evidence and constraints.

### 4. Comparative historical interpretation

The conventional model explains functional ERV-derived elements through retroviral insertion, retention, exaptation, purifying selection and lineage-specific regulatory innovation. Orthologous insertion sites, matching flanks, shared mutations and nested genomic distributions provide strong evidence for inheritance in well-sampled clades. A design-friendly account that permits inheritance should acknowledge this evidence.

The OBN proposal differs principally in its initial conditions. It permits multiple originally functionally mature lineages and a hypothesized original biological network. To become competitive, it must specify lineage boundaries independently of the ERV evidence under test, restrict possible initial placements, and predict signatures that are more likely under its distinctive assumptions than under evolutionary recruitment.

**Current comparative judgment:** Functional ERV examples demonstrate mechanism and compatibility. They do not currently favor original placement over experimentally and phylogenetically supported co-option histories.

### 5. Prospective research programme

| Test | Independently specified requirement | Discriminating observation | Important alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1: Original module architecture | Define expected module functions, regulatory targets and host compatibility before locus selection | Enrichment of specified modules in independently delimited initial lineages beyond calibrated null | Convergent selection, co-option, sequence constraints |
| T2: Insertion-age congruence | Specify expected age/distribution pattern under P1 vs P2 | Synteny, flanking repeats, shared derived mutations and phylogenetic timing | Vertical inheritance and repeated insertion |
| T3: Network integration | Predict quantitative coordination among putative modules and regulatory targets | Perturbation-validated network structure exceeding null expectations | Evolved regulatory networks and selection |
| T4: Cross-lineage viral history | Define exact sublineage signatures and non-primate comparators | Confirmed A/B presence and true absence with equivalent sensitivity | Host-range shifts, horizontal transmission, deletion |
| T5: Functional classification | Pre-register causal phenotype thresholds and negative controls | Reproducible perturbation effects for a restricted predicted class | Biochemical noise and secondary effects |

The mere discovery of additional functional noncoding DNA is a **shared expectation**, not a distinctive success for OBN. A valid discriminating test must establish a differential prediction.

### 6. Research safeguards and possible failure

1. Never infer design directly from present function or complexity.
2. Do not assign arbitrary original placements to rescue a contradictory locus.
3. Distinguish ERV family similarity, subfamily identity and orthologous insertion.
4. Register lineage boundaries and predictions independently of selected examples.
5. Retain nonfunctional, deleterious and contradictory genomic cases.
6. Treat shared orthologous insertions and shared derived mutations as strong inheritance constraints.
7. Report sequence assembly gaps and inadequate negative sampling as unresolved.
8. If restricted P1 predictions repeatedly fail while P2 explains the same observations, lower confidence in the initial-placement component.
9. Keep the OBN programme independent of parallel research tracks subject to the existing research firewall.

### 7. Conclusions

The strongest defensible design-friendly inference from formerly classified “junk” DNA is that genomic systems contain experimentally demonstrated functional complexity, including regulatory roles for particular ERV-derived elements. These discoveries strengthen the biological plausibility of sophisticated information-handling architectures and justify investigation of alternative initial conditions.

They do not demonstrate that a given element was present at creation. They do not invalidate common ancestry or conventional evolutionary co-option. They also do not require that every ERV be a remnant of a once-beneficial system.

**The OBN's distinctive scientific task is to convert the proposal of original functional genomic architecture into independently constrained predictions that can outperform, or fail against, competing historical reconstructions.** This whitepaper establishes the conceptual and experimental starting point for that work.
