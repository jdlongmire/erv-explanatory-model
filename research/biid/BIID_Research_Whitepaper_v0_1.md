# Biological Information Infrastructure Degradation (BIID)
## A Functional-Origin Hypothesis for Viral Delivery Systems and Endogenous Retroviral Remnants
**Working paper v0.1 | 2026-11-16 | Exploratory, unreviewed**

### Abstract
BIID proposes that some viral delivery machinery and endogenous retroviral (ERV) sequences may derive from an originally regulated, beneficial inter-organism genetic information-delivery infrastructure, later affected by changes in regulation, host targeting and replication. The model separates BIID-A (ancestral functional delivery) from BIID-B (a specific account of orthologous ERV loci). Demonstrated biological mechanisms are precedents, not evidence that the proposed historical sequence occurred.

### 1. Proposed architecture
The hypothesized infrastructure includes (1) payload specification, (2) packaging and protection, (3) recipient recognition, (4) transport, (5) integration or regulated expression, and (6) validation and lifecycle control. In the proposed initially good ecosystem, normal aging and turnover remain possible. Hypothesized degradation modes include target mismatch, replication escape, insertional disruption, loss of host compatibility and exploitation.

### 2. Established anchors and limitations
- *Rhodobacter capsulatus* gene-transfer agents package bacterial DNA and facilitate transfer. These are regulated, virus-related systems, but their history is often interpreted as viral machinery adapted to beneficial host-mediated exchange.
- Retrovirus-derived *syncytin-A* is essential for normal placental development in mice, demonstrating present biological function rather than originally beneficial viral ancestry.
- Retroviral genomic integration is biased by host and viral factors. Bias alone does not explain precise shared orthologous insertions.
- ERV genomic patterns include orthologous insertion sites, target-site duplications, shared disabling mutations and nested phylogenetic distributions. BIID must explain these at least as well as vertical inheritance from common ancestors.
- Horizontal retroviral transmission is established and must be distinguished from an ancestral regulated cross-species delivery system.

### 3. Competing historical models
**M1, conventional:** Germline retroviral integration, inheritance through descent, lineage-specific insertion and loss, selection, and occasional horizontal transmission.

**M2, BIID:** Original beneficial delivery architecture, with ancestral regulated distribution followed by degradation and subsequent inheritance.

**M3, hybrid:** Beneficial delivery mechanisms existed in some contexts, while most or all observed orthologous ERV patterns follow M1. The beneficial systems could themselves have been recruited from parasitic machinery.

### 4. Proposed tests
Create a locus-level evidence matrix for HERV-W, HERV-K (HML-2) and PTERV1. For each locus record genome assembly and coordinates, reference sequence, orthology and flanking synteny, target-site duplications, LTR divergence, disabling mutations, species distribution, functional assays and provenance. Pre-register what distinct predictions BIID makes that M1 does not already explain. Model comparisons must penalize unconstrained auxiliary assumptions and specify uncertainty.

**Potential falsification:** BIID-B loses explanatory support if it cannot generate testable predictions distinguishing it from inheritance and horizontal infection, or if its predictions systematically contradict independently verified loci. BIID-A is weakened if no credible molecular lineage supports the proposed beneficial-to-parasitic direction and all examined candidate mechanisms favor reverse co-option.

### 5. Evidence status
- **HIGH, experimentally demonstrated:** Regulated gene-transfer agents; essential syncytin-A function; nonrandom retroviral integration mechanisms.
- **HIGH, comparative evidence:** Many shared ERV loci are consistent with common ancestry and vertical inheritance.
- **UNCERTAIN:** A common originally beneficial infrastructure ancestral to the ERV groups under examination.
- **UNCERTAIN:** Degradation as the historical source of pathogenic viral forms.
